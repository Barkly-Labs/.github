### Human-centered technology, open systems, and creative engineering.

# 🧠 Our Philosophy

Technology is often designed around what computers can do.

We want to start somewhere different:

**What do humans need?**

People have different abilities, energy levels, experiences,
responsibilities, environments, and ways of interacting with technology.

A system that works perfectly for one person may create unnecessary
barriers for another.

So we try to build systems that are:

### 🐾 Human-centered

People are not machines.

Technology should account for real human needs rather than expecting
everyone to behave exactly the same way.

### ♿ Accessible

Accessibility should not be something added at the very end.

It should influence how systems are designed from the beginning.

### 🧩 Understandable

People should be able to understand what a system is doing,
why it is doing it, and how they can interact with it.

### 🔓 Open

Whenever practical, we believe in sharing knowledge, documentation,
experiments, and tools so other people can learn from the work.

### 🛠️ Experimental

Not every idea needs to be perfect before it can exist.

We prototype, test, learn, document what happened, and improve.

### 🛡️ Respectful

Technology should respect human autonomy, privacy, creativity,
and the ability of people to make their own decisions.

```text

  ↓

  ↓
PROTOTYPE
  ↓
TEST
  ↓
BUILD
  ↓

  ↓

  ↓

  ↓
```

# 🧑‍💻 Engineering for Humans

We believe good engineering is not only about performance,
complexity, or technical capability.

It is also about the experience of the person using the system.

That means thinking about:

- Cognitive load
- Accessibility
- Clear interfaces
- Error recovery
- Documentation
- Privacy
- Maintainability
- Reliability
- Flexibility
- Human attention
- Real-world constraints

A technically impressive system that makes people's lives harder
has missed part of the problem.

# 🌎 Technology in the Real World

Barkly Labs is interested in technology that can exist outside
a laboratory.

That means asking questions like:

> Can a normal person understand this?

> Can someone use it when they are tired?

> What happens when something goes wrong?

> Can another developer understand the code?

> Can the system work without unnecessary complexity?

> Can people adapt the technology to their needs?

These questions are just as important as the technical ones.

# 🐾 Our Standard

When we build something, we try to ask:

```text
Does this help people?

Is it understandable?

Is it accessible?

Is it respectful?

Can people actually use it?

Can someone else learn from it?

Does the technology serve the human?

Would we want to use this ourselves?
```

These questions are not a checklist that makes a project perfect.

They are a direction.
